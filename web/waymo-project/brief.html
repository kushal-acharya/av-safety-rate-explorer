What are we trying
to find out?

Whether selected published safety comparisons can be reproduced—and how much their conclusions depend on the reference we choose.

Imagine comparing two drivers.

One drives a short distance on quiet roads. The other drives millions of miles through a busy city. Counting their crashes alone would not be a fair comparison. We also need the distance driven, the driving conditions and the uncertainty in the estimates.

This project applies that reasoning to public Waymo research. It reproduces selected calculations and lets you explore how the human comparison changes when we account for where Waymo drove.

What this project gives you

A way to inspect evidence and question assumptions. It is a statistical research tool, not a self-driving system, and it does not prove that Waymo is safer in every situation.

Five words you’ll see

Exposure
How much driving took place, measured here in miles.
Rate
Events divided by miles. It lets us compare different amounts of driving.
Benchmark
The human reference rate we compare Waymo with.
Rate ratio
Waymo’s rate divided by the human reference. Below 1 means a lower observed rate.
Uncertainty interval
A range calculated under a statistical model. Its meaning depends on which sources of uncertainty the model includes.
